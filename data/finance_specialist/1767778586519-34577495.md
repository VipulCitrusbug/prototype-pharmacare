# User Role: Revenue Cycle Manager (RCM Lead)

## **User Role: Revenue Cycle Manager (RCM Lead)**

### **Detailed Role Overview**

The **Revenue Cycle Manager (RCM Lead)** is the **strategic owner of revenue integrity** across the entire billing lifecycle—from charge capture to final cash realization. This role does **not** work claim by claim. Instead, it operates at a **system, payer, and performance level**, ensuring that revenue leakage is minimized, denials are prevented upstream, and operational workflows are continuously optimized.

The RCM Lead’s primary question is not *“What failed?”* but:

* *“Where are we about to lose revenue?”*

* *“Which patterns are emerging across payers and teams?”*

* *“Are we collecting what we should—and fast enough?”*

The platform positions the RCM Lead as a **decision-maker and orchestrator**, with AI acting as a **forward-looking early warning system**, not an automation engine.

---

### **Core Objectives**

From an RCM Lead’s perspective, the system must:

* **Predict and prevent denials**, not just report them

* Provide **clear financial foresight**, not lagging indicators

* Highlight **systemic workflow issues**, not individual blame

* Support **data-backed leadership decisions**

---

### **Primary Responsibilities**

1. **Denial Prevention & Risk Oversight**

   * Monitor denial risk *before* claim submission

   * Identify systemic denial drivers (payer, code category, documentation gaps)

   * Intervene upstream via process or training changes

2. **Revenue Performance Management**

   * Track net collections, AR aging, and cash velocity

   * Forecast near-term and mid-term revenue

   * Identify revenue leakage and recovery opportunities

3. **Operational Workflow Optimization**

   * Monitor end-to-end RCM workflows

   * Detect bottlenecks, SLA risks, and workload imbalances

   * Align staffing and throughput with claim volume

4. **Cross-Functional Leadership**

   * Coordinate with billing, coding, clinical documentation, and finance teams

   * Set priorities based on risk and financial impact

   * Provide leadership-level insights to executives

---

### **AI Role & Boundaries (RCM Lead-Facing)**

AI is **high-impact but strictly advisory** for this role.

---

#### **A. Denial Risk Intelligence**

AI continuously analyzes:

* historical denial patterns

* payer-specific rules

* coding/documentation correlations

to surface:

* **pre-submission denial risk heatmaps**

* clustered root causes (payer, CPT/ICD, documentation)

* emerging trend deviations

AI does **not** auto-block or auto-correct claims.

---

#### **B. Revenue Performance Intelligence**

AI models:

* net collection trajectories

* AR aging movement

* cash flow forecasts

to provide:

* forward-looking revenue projections

* “at-risk” revenue visibility

* scenario-based insight (trend continuation vs intervention)

---

#### **C. Workflow Optimization Intelligence**

AI evaluates:

* claim throughput

* queue dwell times

* staffing vs workload patterns

to flag:

* bottlenecks before SLAs are breached

* workload mismatches

* operational stress points

AI does **not** reassign work or change staffing autonomously.

---

### **What the RCM Lead Does *Not* Do in the System**

* No line-level claim edits

* No direct patient communication

* No clinical documentation editing

* No AI model tuning

---

### **Design & Governance Principles**

* AI insights are **explainable and drill-down capable**

* All recommendations are **non-binding**

* Metrics are **aggregated, role-safe, and auditable**

* Focus on **systems, not individuals**

---

### **Value Delivered**

* Fewer preventable denials

* Faster cash realization

* Reduced AR backlog

* Clear leadership visibility into revenue health

# User Role: Billing & Coding Specialist

## **User Role: Billing & Coding Specialist**

### **Detailed Role Overview**

The **Billing & Coding Specialist** is the **precision executor** of the Revenue Cycle. This role sits at the **point of conversion between clinical care and financial realization**, where accuracy, speed, and compliance directly determine whether revenue is **collected cleanly or lost through denials and rework**.

The system is designed to **support—not replace—human expertise**. AI here acts as a **co-pilot**, reducing cognitive load, catching gaps early, and improving first-pass acceptance rates, while the specialist retains **full control and accountability** over final submissions.

The Billing & Coding Specialist’s key questions are:

* *“Is this claim clean before I send it?”*

* *“Am I missing anything that could cause a denial?”*

* *“Can I code this faster without sacrificing accuracy?”*

---

### **Core Objectives**

From this role’s perspective, the platform must:

* Maximize **first-pass claim acceptance**

* Reduce **manual rework and denials**

* Improve **coding consistency and confidence**

* Maintain **payer-specific compliance**

---

### **Primary Responsibilities**

1. **Medical Coding**

   * Assign ICD-10, CPT, and HCPCS codes

   * Ensure coding aligns with clinical documentation

   * Apply payer-specific coding rules

2. **Claim Preparation**

   * Review documentation completeness

   * Validate modifiers, units, and sequencing

   * Prepare claims for submission

3. **Quality Assurance**

   * Catch errors before submission

   * Resolve documentation gaps

   * Minimize downstream denials

4. **Collaboration**

   * Coordinate with Clinical Documentation Specialists

   * Respond to RCM Lead guidance on denial trends

   * Escalate unclear documentation issues appropriately

---

### **AI Role & Boundaries (Billing & Coding-Facing)**

AI in this role is **highly tactical and scoped**, focused on **accuracy and speed**, never autonomy.

---

#### **A. Autonomous Coding Assistant**

AI analyzes clinical notes to:

* suggest ICD-10 / CPT / HCPCS codes

* highlight likely primary vs secondary codes

* provide **confidence scores per code**

* learn from accepted edits over time

**AI does**

* accelerate coding  
   **AI does NOT**

* auto-finalize codes

* submit claims

---

#### **B. Documentation Gap Intelligence**

AI scans documentation to:

* flag missing or weak elements (e.g., laterality, severity)

* suggest clarification prompts (non-clinical)

* identify denial-risk documentation patterns

**AI does NOT**

* rewrite clinical notes

* interpret medical intent

---

#### **C. Smart Claim Scrubbing AI**

Before submission, AI checks:

* payer-specific rules

* modifier correctness

* coverage constraints

to suggest:

* corrections

* warnings

* high-risk flags

**AI does NOT**

* block submission

* override user decisions

---

### **What This Role Does *Not* Do**

* No financial forecasting

* No patient communication

* No AI model tuning

* No workflow configuration

---

### **Design & Governance Principles**

* Human-in-the-loop always

* Confidence scoring over black-box rules

* Explainable payer logic

* Auditability at every step

---

### **Value Delivered**

* Higher first-pass acceptance rates

* Fewer preventable denials

* Faster coding throughput

* Reduced specialist burnout

## **User Role: Billing & Coding Specialist**

### **Detailed User Flow**

This user flow describes how a **Billing & Coding Specialist** moves through the system to **code accurately, prepare clean claims, and reduce denial risk**, with AI acting as a **real-time quality and efficiency assistant**, not an automated decision-maker.

The flow is **task-focused, sequential, and interruption-aware**, matching real-world billing operations.

---

## **1\. Secure Access & Work Context Initialization**

* Specialist logs in via secure credentials (SSO / MFA).

* System loads:

  * assigned work queues (encounters / charts)

  * payer mix and rule context

  * specialty-specific coding guidelines

* AI models preload payer rules and documentation patterns.

---

## **2\. Work Queue Overview (Landing Screen)**

* Specialist lands on **Coding & Billing Queue**, showing:

  * pending encounters

  * priority flags (aging, SLA risk)

  * denial-risk indicators (pre-submission)

* AI highlights **which charts deserve extra attention**, not how to code them.

This answers:  
 **“What should I work on first?”**

---

## **3\. Chart Selection & Initial Review**

* Specialist opens an encounter.

* System displays:

  * patient encounter summary

  * provider documentation

  * prior coding history (if applicable)

---

## **4\. AI-Assisted Code Suggestions**

### **AI Touchpoint: Autonomous Coding Assistant**

* AI suggests:

  * ICD-10 diagnoses

  * CPT / HCPCS procedures

  * modifiers (where relevant)

* Each suggestion includes:

  * confidence score

  * explanation (documentation source)

Specialist:

* accepts

* edits

* rejects suggestions

---

## **5\. Documentation Gap Detection**

### **AI Touchpoint: Documentation Gap Intelligence**

* AI flags:

  * missing specificity

  * weak or ambiguous documentation

  * denial-prone omissions

* Suggestions are phrased as:

  * “Clarification needed”

  * “Potential denial risk”

Specialist may:

* proceed with caution

* flag for documentation follow-up

* coordinate with Clinical Documentation Specialist

---

## **6\. Claim Assembly & Validation**

* Specialist assembles the claim:

  * code sequencing

  * units

  * modifiers

* Manual review remains mandatory.

---

## **7\. Pre-Submission Claim Scrubbing**

### **AI Touchpoint: Smart Claim Scrubbing AI**

* AI checks claim against:

  * payer-specific rules

  * coverage limits

  * common denial patterns

* AI presents:

  * warnings

  * fix suggestions

  * risk indicators

Specialist decides whether to:

* revise claim

* submit as-is (with awareness)

---

## **8\. Submission Readiness Review**

* Specialist performs final check:

  * documentation completeness

  * coding alignment

  * AI risk flags reviewed

* System requires explicit confirmation before submission.

---

## **9\. Claim Submission**

* Claim is submitted to payer (via clearinghouse / integration).

* Submission timestamp and metadata are logged.

---

## **10\. Post-Submission Monitoring (Read-Only)**

* Specialist can view:

  * claim status

  * acceptance / rejection notices

* No automated rework is triggered.

---

## **11\. Feedback & Learning Loop**

* AI tracks:

  * accepted vs denied claims

  * specialist edits vs AI suggestions

* Learning occurs **passively**, without changing rules mid-stream.

---

## **12\. Collaboration & Escalation (As Needed)**

* Specialist flags:

  * repeated documentation issues

  * unclear provider notes

* Escalations go to:

  * Clinical Documentation Specialist

  * RCM Lead (pattern-level)

---

## **13\. Session End**

* Specialist returns to queue or logs out.

* All actions are logged for auditability.

---

### **Key Operating Principles**

* Human validation at every step

* AI explains, never enforces

* Accuracy over speed—but speed improves naturally

* Audit-ready by design

# User Role: Patient Financial Services

## **User Role: Patient Financial Services / Financial Counselor**

### **Detailed Role Overview**

The **Patient Financial Services / Financial Counselor** is the **bridge between complex healthcare billing and patient understanding**. This role exists to ensure that patients **clearly understand their financial responsibility**, feel supported rather than surprised, and are guided toward **timely, realistic payment outcomes**.

Unlike billing or coding roles, this user operates in a **patient-facing, trust-sensitive context**. The goal is not revenue extraction—it is **financial clarity, fairness, and reduced bad debt**, achieved through transparency and proactive guidance.

The system is designed to help the Financial Counselor answer:

* *“What will this patient likely owe?”*

* *“How likely are they to pay on time?”*

* *“What is the best way to communicate with them?”*

AI here functions as a **decision-support and communication optimization layer**, never as an automated collections engine.

---

### **Core Objectives**

From this role’s perspective, the platform must:

* Provide **accurate, explainable cost estimates**

* Reduce **patient confusion and disputes**

* Increase **payment completion rates**

* Maintain **patient trust and compliance**

---

### **Primary Responsibilities**

1. **Financial Responsibility Explanation**

   * Explain estimated patient out-of-pocket costs

   * Clarify insurance coverage and limits

   * Set expectations before or after care

2. **Payment Planning**

   * Offer appropriate payment plan options

   * Align payment schedules with patient ability

   * Reduce likelihood of delinquency

3. **Patient Communication**

   * Engage patients empathetically

   * Send reminders and follow-ups at appropriate times

   * Address billing questions and concerns

4. **Coordination with RCM & Billing**

   * Align patient communications with claim status

   * Escalate recurring coverage or affordability issues

   * Feed insights back to RCM leadership

---

### **AI Role & Boundaries (Financial Counselor-Facing)**

AI is used **only to improve clarity, timing, and likelihood of success**, not to pressure or automate collections.

---

#### **A. Financial Responsibility Prediction**

AI evaluates:

* insurance coverage data

* plan limitations

* historical patient responsibility patterns

to estimate:

* likely out-of-pocket cost ranges

* coverage exhaustion risk

Estimates are always presented as **ranges with confidence indicators**.

---

#### **B. Payment Behavior Prediction**

AI analyzes:

* historical payment patterns (aggregated, compliant)

* demographic and plan-level signals

to flag:

* likelihood of delayed or missed payments

* recommended payment plan structures

AI does **not** score or label patients negatively.

---

#### **C. Communication Optimization AI**

AI assists with:

* best timing for reminders

* recommended communication tone

* channel preference insights

All messages are **human-approved before sending**.

---

### **What This Role Does *Not* Do**

* No claim submission or coding

* No automated collections

* No coercive messaging

* No AI-driven patient decisions

---

### **Design & Governance Principles**

* Patient-first, empathetic design

* Transparency over precision

* AI assists, humans decide

* All communications logged and auditable

---

### **Value Delivered**

* Fewer billing disputes

* Higher patient satisfaction

* Improved payment completion

* Reduced bad debt and write-offs
